Overview
Black Death City is a skirmish wargame where warbands of vying factions battle in the darkness of a ruined city
for glory and fortune.

RECRUIT A WARBAND
In Black Death City, each player takes command of a warband of about a dozen fighters. Every warband hails
from one of the setting’s different factions and has a unique roster of fighters and wargear to choose from. Players
begin with 1000 silver with which to recruit their initial warband.

FIGHT BATTLES
Once a player has recruited their warband, they are ready to fight battles against other warbands as they embark
within the City in hopes of finding riches hidden amidst the ruins. Each battle is fought to a different scenario that
details how the battlefield and warbands are set-up, and most importantly, what each warband must do to win!

EXPLORE THE CITY
These battles are connected into an ongoing narrative called a campaign, where the outcome of each battle affects
the next. Your warband will grow and change over the course of the campaign. Fighters may gain experience and
new skills. They may suffer permanent injuries or even be killed and removed from your warband. In addition, each
warband will vie for control of the City’s many holdings, with which they can earn more silver to recruit new
fighters and purchase additional wargear with.

What you will need...
To play Black Death City, you will need the following:
•

Miniatures: The fighters in a player’s warband are represented
by miniatures. The game is designed for miniatures
25mm-30mm in scale. Once a player has picked which faction
their warband hails from, they can use any suitable miniatures
from their collection to represent the fighters. Human-sized
fighters should be mounted on a base 25mm to 40mm in size.
Larger fighters (such as Mercenary Ogres and other brutes)
might be mounted on a base 40mm to 60mm in size. Bases can
be either circular or square.

•

A Battlefield and Terrain: The game is designed to be played
on a battlefield 36 inches by 36 inches (3 feet by 3 feet) in size,
but can also work on a battlefield 30 inches by 44 inches in size.
Both are standard sizes used in other games and a wide variety of
battlefield mats can be found for purchase.
Black Death City is a place of twisting streets, crumbling ruins
and burned down buildings. Ruins in a late medieval style best
suit the theme of the game.

•

A Warband Roster. Each player will need a completed
Warband Roster that details the particulars of their warband.
You can find a blank warband roster on pages xx-xx.

•

A tape measure in inches. All measurements are in inches
(denoted with ").

•

6-sided dice. We recommend a dozen for each player.

•

A direction dice. This is a dice with arrows upon its faces to
denote a random direction when rolled. If you do not have one
available, you can use a 12-sided dice instead (pg xx).

•

2 templates. The game uses 2 templates, the blast template and
the flame template.
The blast template is used for explosive detonations such as
bombs and for certain arcane spells. This is a circular template 3"
in diameter.
The flame template is used for fire attacks. It is a teardrop shape
8" in length.

How to use this book...
The book is broken down into several chapters to make it easier to navigate:

Chapter 1 - Core Concepts
The first chapter explains the basic principles of the game. How measuring works, the different types of dice rolls,
the values of a fighter, and other fundamentals.

Chapter 2 - Battles
This chapter details the rules for fighting battles, including how the rounds of a battle are played through, how
players take turns activating fighters from their warband, and how fighters can move and make actions, such as
attacking or casting a spell.

Chapter 3 - Scenarios
Every battle is fought to the specifics of a scenario, for example rival warbands may be searching for treasure in an
area of the City, or one warband might have ambushed the other. This chapter details all the scenarios your
warband can find themselves in.

Chapter 4 - Warbands
This chapter focuses on recruiting your warband and detailing all the different options available to each faction.

Chapter 5 - Campaigns
The battles that you fight are linked together into an ongoing story called a campaign. In the practical sense, this
means after every battle both players resolve a series of steps called the aftermath sequence. This sequence
determines things such as any injuries your fallen fighters suffer, the experience and new skills they earn, as well as
calculating the income your warband receives from its holdings in the City.

Chapter 6 - Reference
The final chapter is filled with quick reference tables and charts, to create a useful resource when fighting battles.

Core Concepts
The City is a perilous place to tread, with dangers lurking
down each of its darkened streets. Before you can set foot
within this forsaken domain, you must first learn the
ropes.
This chapter explains the basic principles of Black Death City, including how
distances are measured, the sorts of dice rolls the players will make, what a fighter
is and what wargear they can be equipped with.

WARBANDS & FIGHTERS
In Black Death City each player commands a warband. Warbands are made up
of fighters. Every fighter is represented by an appropriate miniature.

FRIENDLY AND ENEMY FIGHTERS
Many rules refer to friendly fighters and enemy fighters. A player considers
all fighters in their warband to be friendly fighters and all fighters in other
warbands to be enemy fighters. Likewise, a fighter considers all fighters in their
warband to be friendly fighters, and all fighters in other warbands to be enemy
fighters.
If a rule for a fighter refers to “other friendly fighters” or “another friendly
fighter” , it means fighters from that fighter’s warband, excluding itself.

MEASURING DISTANCES
Distances are measured in inches ("). During battle you can measure any distance
whenever you wish.
When measuring between two fighters, use the closest points of the bases of the
fighters you are measuring to and from. Usually, measuring distances will take
into account both the vertical and horizontal distance between two points, but in
some cases a rule might specify to measure the horizontal distance or the
vertical distance only.

THE BATTLEFIELD

d6x2

Battles are intended to be fought on a battlefield either
36" by 36" in size (3 foot by 3 foot), or 30" by 44" in
size.

Another common dice roll is d6x2 or a similar dice roll
that uses multiplication (such as 2d6x5). For such dice
rolls, first roll the dice, then multiply the result by the
number shown. For example if the rule called for
d6x2", a dice roll of a 5 would result in 10 inches.

The battlefield should be populated with terrain
features to create a ruined district of the City. Players
work together to create a thematic battlefield. The
rules for terrain are detailed on pg xx.

The Battlefield vs The Battlefield Floor
If a rule specifies the battlefield it includes the
terrain features and any platforms upon them. For
example, if a treasure token needs to be set up
“anywhere on the battlefield” it could be set up on a
platform such as the upper story of a ruined building.
If a rule specifies the battlefield floor, it means the
ground floor of the battlefield only.

ROLL-OFFS
When the rules asks the players to roll off, each
player rolls a dice and whoever rolls highest wins the
roll-off. If there is a tie for the highest roll, make the
roll-off again.

RE-ROLLS
Some rules allow you to re-roll a dice roll (for
example, when making an attack roll), which means
you get to roll some or all of the dice again. The rule
that allows the re-roll will specify exactly how many of
the dice can be re-rolled. A dice roll can never be
re-rolled more than once.

SCATTERING

DICE
Black Death City uses 2 types of dice. Standard
six-sided dice and direction dice (a dice with an
arrow on each of its faces.)
Standard six-sided dice are referred to as either a d6
or as dice. If a rule refers to 2d6, 3d6 and so on – roll
that many dice and add the results together. If a rule
requires a dice roll of a certain number or higher, for
example 3 or more, this is abbreviated to 3+.

Modifiers and Natural Rolls

When a rule calls for something to scatter, it means it
moves in a random direction. When something
scatters, determine the direction by rolling the
direction dice then move it horizontally the specified
amount.
There are additional rules for fighters scattering
(pg xx) and blast templates scattering (pg xx)
Designer's Note: Some direction dice have a face
marked ‘HIT’. These faces are not used any
differently, use the small arrow above HIT to
determine the direction.
If you have no direction dice, a twelve-sided dice (d12)
can be used instead. Nominate a battlefield edge to be
“12 o’ clock” and roll the d12, using the hours of a clock
to determine the direction.

Dice rolls are often modified in some way. For
example, the Unwieldy weapon trait subtracts 1 from
attack rolls. This means a roll of a 4 becomes a 3, a roll
of a 5 becomes a 4 and so on.
If a rule refers to a natural roll, it means the dice roll
without considering any modifiers. For example, when
making attack rolls (pg xx), natural rolls of 6 are
critical hits. This means if the dice rolled shows a 6,
it is a critical hit regardless of any modifiers.

d3
To roll a d3, roll a six-sided dice and halve the total,
rounding up. For example, a roll of a 3 or 4 on the dice
would be a 2.

d66
To roll a d66, roll a six-sided dice twice. The first roll
determines the ten, and the second roll determines the
unit. For example, if you rolled a 3 followed by a 5, the
d66 roll would be 35.

VISIBILITY

SEQUENCING

Often you will need to determine if a fighter is visible
to another.

In most cases, rules will be resolved one at a time.
However, from time to time, rules may appear to come
into play at the same time and it may be unclear as to
which is resolved first. If this occurs during a round,
the player whose turn it is to activate a fighter chooses
the order in which the rules are resolved. In any other
case, the players roll off and the winner chooses.

A fighter is visible if a straight line can be drawn from
any part of its body (head, torso or limbs), to the
observing fighter’s head, without the line passing
through solid terrain or another fighter. The direction
the observing fighter is facing does not matter, all
fighters are presumed to have a 360° field of view.
If the only parts of a fighter visible to the observing
fighter are its weapons, banners, feathers, spikes or
tail, the fighter is not considered visible.
Sometimes the rules for visibility will refer to a point
on the battlefield (eg. “Pick a visible point on the
battlefield.”) This is determined in the same manner; a
point on the battlefield is visible to a fighter if a
straight line can be drawn from it to the observing
fighter’s head, without the line passing through solid
terrain or another fighter.
Lastly, fighters are visible to themselves, (for example,
if a rule says, “Pick a visible fighter within 6" of this
fighter.” The fighter the rule is referring to could be
picked.)

Obscured
If some parts of a fighter’s body (head, torso or limbs)
are visible, but other parts are hidden from view to the
observing fighter, either due to intervening solid
terrain or another fighter, that fighter is obscured
from the observer. Obscured fighters are in cover
from ranged attacks (pg xx).

This fighter is visible to the observing fighter,
however as part of its body is hidden from
view due to the intervening wall, this fighter is
obscured to the observing fighter.

VALUES

Initiative

Every fighter has 7 values: Move, Blade, Bow,
Armour, Initiative, Willpower and Life.

A fighter’s Initiative value determines how quickly they
can think on their feet in the heat of battle. The higher
the number the better. This value is added to a dice roll
to determine who strikes first in melee attacks, it is
also used to test for falling.

Move
A fighter’s Move value determines the maximum
distance a fighter can travel across the battlefield when
making a Move. The higher the number the better. A
typical human has a Move value of 5".

Blade
A fighter’s Blade value determines how skilled they are
with melee weapons. The lower the number the better.
The value indicates the dice roll needed to score a hit
with a melee weapon. For example, when the Guild
Wizard makes a melee attack, each roll of 4 or more
scores a hit.

Bow
Similar to the Blade value, a fighter’s Bow value
determines how skilled they are with ranged weapons.
The lower the number the better. The value indicates
the dice roll needed to score a hit with a ranged
weapon. For example, when the Guild Wizard makes a
ranged attack, only rolls of a 6 or more score a hit,
meaning they are not very skilled with ranged
weapons.

Armour
A fighter’s Armour value determines how likely enemy
attacks will be deflected by their armour, keeping the
fighter safe from harm. The lower the number the
better. A value of 6+ represents minimal armour (such
as robes or padded cloth). A value of 3+ represents
heavy armour with a shield.
Most fighters will have an Armour value of ‘6+’ when
recruited to your warband. You will have the option to
improve their Armour value by equipping them with
armour and shields (see pg xx).

Improving and Worsening Armour
If a rule states the Armour value is improved by 1,
the number is decreased by the amount (eg. 6+
becomes 5+). Likewise if it is worsened by 1, the
number is increased by the amount (eg. 5+
becomes 6+).
An Armour value of 2+ cannot be improved
further. If an Armour value of 6+ is worsened it
becomes '-' (which means no armour roll is made,
pg xx). An Armour value of '-' cannot be worsened
further.

11

Willpower
A fighter’s Willpower value determines their mental
fortitude, grit and determination. It also determines
their skills with the arcane, their piety and their faith.
The higher the number the better. This value is often
used in conjunction with casting spells.

Initiative & Willpower Tests
Sometimes the rules will require a test to be made
against a fighter’s Initiative or Willpower value. To
do so, the commanding player rolls a dice hoping
for a low number. If the score is equal or less
than the value being tested, the fighter passes the
test. Otherwise they fail.

Life
A fighter’s Life value determines how much damage
they can sustain in battle before they are laid low by
their wounds. The higher the number the better. A
typical human has a Life value of 4.

Life, Damage, Healing &
Bloodied
As your warband battles against its rivals, both your
fighters and their enemies will suffer damage.
Fighters can suffer damage from all manner of
sources, falling from height, carrying cursed treasure,
but most often it will come from being hit by an
attack.
During the battle you will need to keep track of how
much life each of your fighters has. At the start of the
battle this equals the fighter’s Life value, but each
time they suffer damage, their life is reduced by an
amount equal to the damage.
Some rules, such as healing potions or spells, may
heal a fighter. Healing a fighter restores life reduced
by damage, but a fighter’s life can never exceed their
Life value.

When a fighter’s life is reduced to zero they are taken
down and removed from the battlefield. The fighter
hasn’t necessarily been killed, after the battle an
injury roll is made for them to determine their fate
(see pg xx).

Bloodied
While a fighter has only 1 point of life remaining, they
are bloodied and suffer from the following penalty:
Bloodied: Each time a bloodied fighter
activates, they can make a move or an
action, but cannot do both. If they are
healed during the activation, this penalty no
longer applies to that activation.

WEAPON VALUES

Damage and Critical Damage

Range

The Damage value of a weapon determines how many
points of life a fighter loses when they are hit with the
weapon. As detailed later in the rules, attacks may
score critical hits, in which case the Critical Damage
value is used instead.

Melee weapons do not have a Range value, but ranged
weapons do. A weapon’s Range value determines the
maximum distance in inches a target can be for a
ranged attack.

Attacks
A weapon’s Attacks value determines how many dice
are rolled each time the weapon is used for an attack.
For melee weapons, this represents each swing and is
usually 3. For ranged weapons, this represents a shot
of ammunition and is usually 1.

Rend
A weapon’s Rend value represents how efficiently the
weapon can pierce armour. The value is negative and
modifies armour rolls made for the target of an attack.
Improving and Worsening Rend
If a rule states the Rend value is improved by 1, the
negative number is increased by the amount (eg. -1
becomes -2). Likewise if it is worsened by 1, the
negative number is decreased by the amount (eg. -2
becomes -1).

Weapon Traits
Weapons can have 1 or more weapon traits. For
example the Club has the Bludgeon and Off-hand
weapon traits. You can find the full rules for all the
different weapon traits on pages xx-xx.

Terrain

Ladders, Windows And Doors

The battlefield will be populated with various different
terrain features, such as ruined buildings, low walls
and statues.

Deadly Terrain

The different parts of each terrain feature are
categorised into different types of terrain as detailed
on this page.
When the players set up the battlefield, it is
recommended they discuss each terrain feature to
clarify the types of terrain it has.
The types of terrain are: low terrain, high terrain,
platforms, ladders, windows and doors. In
addition, players can agree for certain parts of terrain
features to be deadly terrain or impassable
terrain.

Low Terrain
Eg. Rubble, Low Walls, Fences
Any part of a terrain feature less than 1.5" tall is low
terrain. How fighters move across low terrain is
detailed on pg xx.

Terrain might have ladders, windows or doors. These
each have special rules for moving as covered on pg xx.

Spiked walls and fences, bubbling pits of acid and
other such unwholesome places are deadly terrain.
If a fighter moves across or falls onto deadly terrain,
they suffer d3 damage.
Players should agree on what terrain constitutes deadly
terrain when the battlefield is being set up.

Impassable Terrain
Sloped rooftops, statues, fountains, sarcophaguses,
braziers and trees are examples of impassable
terrain.
Players should agree on what terrain constitutes
impassable terrain when the battlefield is being set up.
Fighters cannot climb impassable terrain. They cannot
move across impassable terrain unless they can fly.
They cannot end a move on impassable terrain.
If a fighter is moved onto impassable terrain (for
example, after falling off a platform). They are moved
by the shortest route until they are no longer on it.

High Terrain
Eg. High Walls, Sides of a Building

Terrain Bases

Any wall 1.5" or greater in height is high terrain.
High terrain can be climbed (see pg xx).

Many terrain features come mounted on a base.
The base of a terrain feature is treated as the
battlefield floor.

Platforms
Eg. Upper floors
Any horizontally flat surface on a terrain feature with a
surface area larger than 1" by 1" is a platform. While a
fighter is on a platform, the edge of the miniature’s
base can overhang the edge of the platform, but the
centre of their base must remain on the platform.

Falling Off Platforms
When a fighter within ½" of the edge of an open
platform (i.e. an edge that is not enclosed by a wall
or fence) suffers damage, there is a chance they will
fall.
After the action has been resolved, the fighter must
pass an Initiative test else they are moved off the
platform by the shortest route and fall (see pg xx).
This rule does not affect fighters with the Fly
keyword.

Battles
The inner districts of the City are ruined and lawless.
Warbands must be ready to defend their claims with
blackpowder and steel, bringing swift death to any rival
who dares challenge them for their hard-earned spoils.
This chapter details how battles are fought between two warbands.

SETTING UP THE BATTLE
To set up a battle, follow this sequence:
1. Determine the Underdog
2. Determine the Scenario
3. Follow the Scenario ‘Before the Battle’ Steps

1. Determine the Underdog
Every warband has a warband rating (pg xx), in every battle, the warband with
the lesser Warband Rating is the underdog. During the battle the underdog may
be granted certain bonuses giving them the edge against their rivals to help even
the odds.
In the unlikely event that two warbands have identical warband ratings, the
players roll off and the winner is the underdog for the battle.

2. Determine the Scenario
Every battle is fought to a scenario. The scenario sets up the narrative of the
battle, dictates any twists or special rules in play, and most importantly, what
each warband needs to do to win the battle.
The players roll on the Scenario Table (pg xx) to determine the scenario used
for the battle.
Scenarios are detailed in Chapter 3 (pg xx).

3. Follow the Scenario ‘Before the Battle’ Steps
The Scenario will detail how the battlefield is set up and anything that needs to be
resolved before the battle . Resolve these steps in order.

4. Determine Prepared Spells
If either Warband includes any casters (Wizards or Priests), the spells they have
prepared are now determined (see pg xx).

5. Resolve Any Other ‘Before the Battle’ Rules
If either player has any other special rules that are resolved before the battle, they
are resolved now, starting with the underdog.

6. Begin the Battle
The battle is now ready to begin.

Rounds
Once the battle begins, it is fought in a series of rounds until it ends. Each round follows this sequence:

Round Sequence
1. Priority Roll

- players roll off to determine who goes first (the winner chooses, the
underdog wins ties.)

2. Start of Round Step

- certain rules may trigger in this step, otherwise it is skipped.

3. Fighters Activate

- players alternate activating fighters from their warband, until every
fighter has been activated.
- after all fighters are activated, rules may trigger at the end of the
round.
- warbands with 5 or more fighters bloodied or taken down must take a
Flee test (if both warbands are eligible, the underdog goes first).

4. End of Round Step
5. Flee Test

1. Priority Roll

5. Flee Test

At the start of each round the players roll off. The
winner has priority and decides which player first
activates a fighter. In the case of a tie, the underdog
has priority.

The final step of the round is to make Flee tests. A
Flee test must be made for a warband if 5 or more of
its fighters are either bloodied or taken down.

2. Start of Round Step
After the priority roll, there is the Start of Round step.
This step is for any rules that need to be resolved
before fighters can be activated. The scenario rules are
the most common place to find such rules (for
example, fighters arriving from reserve).
If there are no rules that need to be resolved, the step
is skipped. If there are multiple rules, the player with
priority decides which order they are resolved.

If both warbands must make a Flee test, the underdog
does so first; if they fail the battle ends. If they pass
their opponent then makes a flee test.
To make a Flee test, the player rolls a dice. On a 1, the
Flee test fails, the warband immediately flees and the
battle ends. On a 2+, they pass.
Instead of rolling a dice for a Flee test, a player can
voluntarily choose for their warband to fail the test,
(this can be a wise decision in an unwinnable battle to
minimise further casualties).

3. Fighters Activate
After the Start of Round step, the players begin taking
turns, back and forth, to activate fighters from their
warband to move and make actions. The player with
priority chooses which player takes the first turn. This
is the main part of the round, and where the
excitement takes place. How fighters are activated is
explained in detail throughout the rest of the chapter.

4. End of Round Step
Similar to the Start of Round step, after all fighters
have been activated, any rules that need to be resolved
at the end of a round are done so. If there are no rules
that need to be resolved, the step is skipped. If there
are multiple rules, the player with priority decides
which order they are resolved.

ENDING THE BATTLE
The battle ends either when one of the warbands flees,
or when the victory conditions of the scenario declare
the battle ends.

Activating
Fighters
During each round, the players take turns activating
fighters from their warband. The player with priority
decides which player takes the first turn.
On a player’s turn, they pick a fighter from their
warband to activate, and after the activation has been
resolved, their opponent does the same. This alternates
back and forth between the players until all fighters on
the battlefield have been activated.
Each fighter cannot be activated more than once each
round, (with the exception of surge activations, see
below). In addition, every fighter on the battlefield
must be activated each round. Once all fighters on the
battlefield have been activated the round ends.

1 MOVE & 1 ACTION
When a fighter activates, they can make 1 Move and
1 Action. The order is determined by the commanding
player. For example, they could do their Action first
then their Move, or the other way around. They can
also choose to forgo their Move and/or their Action if
they wish.

SURGE ACTIVATIONS
If a player has no more fighters left to activate in a
round, but their opponent does so, they can start
picking fighters from their warband to make surge
activations.
To make a surge activation, the player picks a fighter
from their warband that has already activated. That
fighter activates for a second time. During that
activation they can make 1 move or 1 action (they
cannot do both).
Bloodied fighters make surge activations as normal.
Each fighter can only be picked to make a surge
activation once each round. If every fighter in a player’s
warband has activated and has also made a surge
activation, but their opponent still has fighters who
have not activated, their opponent activates their
remaining fighters as normal.

BONUS ACTIONS
Certain rules may allow a fighter to make a bonus
action. A common example is using an item. A fighter
can make any number of bonus actions in their
activation in addition to their 1 action. A bloodied
fighter, and a fighter making a surge activation, can
also make as many bonus actions as they are able.

A fighter can make a disengage move while they are

A fighter can make a normal move if they are not

move, they can move across the battlefield a maximum

move within 1" of an enemy fighter while doing so.

While a fighter is within 1" of an enemy fighter,

•

Moving
When a fighter makes any type of move, the following
rules apply:
•

A fighter cannot move through other fighters.

•

A fighter cannot move through any part of a terrain
feature (with the exception of doors and windows,
see below).

•

No part of a fighter can move over the battlefield
edge.

Moving Across Low Terrain
Fighters can move across low terrain (such a rubble
and low walls - see pg xx) without needing to go up and
down, instead subtract 1" from the maximum distance
they can move each time they move across low terrain.

Moving Through Doors and Windows
In a similar manner, a fighter can move through open
windows and doorways even if the space is not
physically big enough for the miniature to move
through. If they do so, subtract 1" from the maximum
distance they can move for each window and doorway
they move through.
Moving Through Closed Doors
A fighter making a normal move can move through
closed doors in the same manner as an open door with
the following exceptions:
•

•

Only fighters making normal moves can move
through a closed door. Fighters making a run,
charge or disengage move cannot.
Beasts cannot move through closed doors.

ADVANCED MOVES
As part of a move, a fighter may jump or climb in
addition to moving across the battlefield. If the fighter
has the Fly keyword, they can fly as well.

Jumping
During a move, a fighter at the edge of a platform can
jump. If they do so, they move off the edge and
continue moving through the air.
Measure the move through the air horizontally. While
in the air, they can move up to 1" vertically (but no
more), and any distance downward vertically. Only the
horizontal distance moved through the air is counted.
The maximum distance a fighter can move horizontally
through the air when jumping is 3".
If a fighter moves 3" or more vertically downwards
while jumping, they suffer impact damage (see
opposite).
In addition, if a fighter ends their move still in the air,
they immediately fall (see below).

FALLING
If a fighter falls, they move vertically downwards
until they are on the battlefield floor or a platform
once more.
If a fighter moves 3" or more vertically downwards
while falling, they suffer impact damage (see
opposite).
If a fighter falls onto other fighters, each fighter
they fall onto is moved outward to make room for
the falling fighter. Move the fighters outward by
the shortest distance until they are more than 1"
from the fighter that fell. Then, each of those
fighters must pass an Initiative test else suffer d3
damage.

When a fighter climbs high terrain, they can move up

High Terrain: If a scattering fighter comes into

Other Fighters: Scattering fighters will stop when

A fighter can finish their activation while climbing high

visibility.
While a fighter is climbing high terrain they cannot

attack action, if any hits are scored, after the attack
action is resolved, the fighter must pass an Initiative
test else they fall.

RESOLVING HITS
To resolve a hit from an attack (be it from a melee
attack, a ranged attack, or an attack spell) the
following sequence is used:

If the target fighter has an Armour value of ‘-’, or the
Rend value of the weapon being used makes a
successful armour roll impossible (eg. an Armour
value of 6+ vs a weapon with a Rend value of -1), no
armour roll needs to be made (the weapon cuts
straight through).

1. Make Armour Rolls
Each time a fighter is hit by an attack, an armour
roll is made for them by rolling a dice. If the result of
the armour roll equals or exceeds their Armour value
the hit has been saved by their armour and is
negated.
For example, if a fighter hit by an attack has an
Armour value of 4+, a dice roll of a 4, 5 or 6 will save
the fighter from the hit.
Rend
Powerful weapons may have a Rend value. If so, each
armour roll is modified by the Rend value of the
weapon being used.
For example, if the target has an Armour value of 4+,
but the weapon has a Rend value of -1, a dice roll of a
5 or 6 will save the fighter from the hit .

25

2. Allocate Damage
For each hit that was not saved by the target’s armour,
the target suffers damage equal to the weapon’s
damage value. If the attack was a critical hit, the
target suffers damage equal to the weapon’s Critical
Damage value instead.

A

Attacks that use the blast template do not need to

B

There are many warbands willing to stake a claim within
the City. Lured like moths to a flame on the promise of
riches hidden beneath the ruins. Most find only death,

Step 3 - Recruit Fighters
Each faction has a list of fighters that can be recruited
to its warbands. Every fighter has one of the following
keywords: Leader, Champion, Henchmen, Brute or
Beast. There are limitations your warband must
adhere to as follows:

1 Leader: Each faction has a variety
of Leaders to choose from. You must
recruit 1 Leader fighter for your
warband. No more, no less.
0-3 Champions: While not as
experienced as your warband’s Leader,
Champions are skilled fighters and
encompass both veteran fighters and
spellcasters such as wizards or priests.
You can include up to 3 Champions in
your warband.
5+ Henchmen: Every faction will
have a range of regular fighters
marked as Henchmen. These will
form the backbone of your warband
and as such you must recruit at least 5
Henchmen.
0-2 Brutes: Large fighters such as
Mercenary Ogres and Gargoyles are
marked as Brutes. A warband can only
include 2 Brutes.
0-3 Beasts: Hunting Dogs, Giant
Rats and other such creatures are
marked as Beasts. A warband can
include up to 3 Beasts.

15 Fighters Limit: A warband can never exceed 15
fighters in total.
Specific Restrictions: Certain fighters might have a
specific restriction next to their name on their fighter
profile. For example the Guild Wizard has ‘0-1’ noted
beside their name on their scroll, meaning that you can
only recruit one such fighter in your warband.
Starting Experience
Each time a fighter is recruited to your warband, they
have a number of starting experience. This is often a
random amount, such as 20+d6. You can find out
more about experience on pages xx-xx.

Step 4 - Buy Wargear
Each faction has wargear lists for its fighters. On
every fighter's scroll, it will detail which wargear lists
they can be given wargear from.

Step 5 - Pick Skills
Certain fighters will allow you to pick skills for them
when they are recruited to your warband. You can find
out more about skills on the next page.

Step 5 - Calculate your Warband Rating
The final step is to calculate the warband rating of
your warband. This is an indicator of how strong your
warband is and is something you will update after each
battle in the campaign.
The warband rating is the combined silver cost of all
the fighters in your warband, the cost of their wargear,
combined with the total experience points of all the
fighters in the warband. Do not include anything in the
stash.

Deep below the City lies a labyrinth
of tunnels and old sewers that is the

uncommon between the different

Held Items
Some items have the Held Item keyword (eg.
Shields). These items count towards the number of
melee weapons a fighter can be equipped with
instead of the number of items. In addition, they
have the following rules:
Two-Handed Melee Weapons: A fighter can be
equipped with a held item and a two-handed melee
weapon (eg. a Spear and Shield), however, subtract 1
from the Attacks value of the fighter's two-handed
melee weapons.
Two-Handed Ranged Weapons: A fighter
cannot be equipped with a held item and a twohanded ranged weapon.

While a fighter is entwined, subtract 1 from the Attacks
value of their melee weapons (off-hand attacks are
unaffected). Each time an entwined fighter is activated,
they can take an Initiative test, if passed, they are no
longer entwined.

The Blighted Cloister are a secretive
monastic order of knights and
sorcerer-priests who believe the
Black Rot to be not a curse but a
blessing bestowed upon the City.
Behind the high stone-walls of their
abbeys they nurse the sick and
disease-ridden with no intention to
cure them. Instead, they are
nurtured into swollen vessels of
pestilence and venerated as living
saints.
Within the Cloister's innermost
circles, their creed has descended
further into blasphemy. Prayers
have given way to dark pacts, and
hymns to whispered invocations.
The Black Rot has drawn the eyes of
demonic entities who seek to walk
the mortal world to sow plague and
disease. These demons grant
strength to the knights of the
Cloister, transforming them into
bloated and rotten creatures that
tower over lesser men. The price is
their soul, and as the demonic
corruption twists their insides there
are few who retain their sanity for
long.
